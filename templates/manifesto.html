Why Did I Made This?

I feel like social media isn't really social anymore. At some point, it stopped being a place where you went to see what your friends were doing and turned into a giant machine for consuming mass-produced content, brainrot, and, increasingly, AI slop. Most of the big platforms are owned by a handful of insanely rich people whose entire business model is basically getting you to stay on their app for as long as possible. Endless scrolling, notifications, recommendations, ads — everything is designed to keep you in a little dopamine loop so you don't close the app. The more time you spend there, the more ads they can show you and the more they can learn about you. And honestly, even before all of that, things like likes and follower counts already made social media kind of weird. Instead of just posting something because you wanted your friends to know what you've been up to, it became a competition. Take the right picture. Say the right thing. Get enough likes. Look like you're having a good life. Wait for people to tell you they like it. Meanwhile, we're all pretty aware by now that spending hours staring at algorithmically optimized feeds probably isn't doing our brains any favors. So I made this. It's just a small place for us to share what we're up to with people who actually care. You don't need to post every day. You don't need to get likes. You don't need to build a following. You don't even really need to use it that often. Maybe you just post something once a month — what you've been doing, a picture from a trip, something dumb that happened, whatever. Then you can check in and see what your friends have been up to too. No algorithm. No endless feed. No likes. No reactions. No ads. Just a little corner of the internet for keeping up with your friends. That's pretty much it.
